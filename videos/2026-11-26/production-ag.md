- **Category:** the human body
- **Topic:** René Laennec, Necker Hospital, Paris, 1816. A young woman with symptoms of heart disease; her build made
  percussion and touch useless, and putting his ear directly on a young woman's chest ("immediate auscultation") was
  improper. He remembered children playing with a long piece of wood — one scratching one end with a pin, the other
  listening at the other end. He rolled a quire of paper into a tight cylinder, put one end on her chest and heard the
  heart more clearly than ever. Later a wooden tube; named "stethoscope" (Greek stethos = chest, skopein = to look/

- **Category:** animal superpowers
- **Topic:** March 1800, near Calabozo, Venezuela. Alexander von Humboldt (with Aimé Bonpland) wanted live electric
  eels. Local men drove about 30 wild horses and mules into a muddy pond; the eels rose, pressed against the horses'
  bellies and discharged repeatedly; within minutes two horses drowned (stunned); others staggered out. Then the eels
