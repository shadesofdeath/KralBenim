  kişi (tahminler 3.500–8.000; emin olunmayan sayı yazılmaz); James Mellaart 1960'larda kazdı; UNESCO 2012.
- Kâğıt: pamuk.
- Yazarın doğrulaması (2026-11-23): National Geographic: evler "kapısız ve penceresiz" sırt sırta, girişler damdaki

  Not: Hodder'ın 1996 raporu en alt katmanlarda damdan girişin tek yol olmayabileceğini söylüyor; bu yüzden metin

## ag-04-pharos-of-alexandria (Stickstory)
- Viewer feels: "One of the Seven Wonders never vanished: it is lying on the seabed."
- Hook + cover: "A wonder of the world lies underwater." ↔ A WONDER **UNDERWATER** (the lit Pharos at night).
- Verified: built more than 2,000 years ago on the island of Pharos (completed c. 280 BC); three tiers, possibly over
  100 m; earthquakes between 956 and 1323; Sultan Qaitbay built a fortress on the site with its stones in 1477;
  1994 Empereur's divers found blocks and statues in the Eastern Harbour.
- Script: docs/ornek/ag-01-pharos-of-alexandria.json (written by the director).

## ag-05-tulip-mania (Stickstory)
- Viewer feels: "The most famous crash in history probably ruined almost nobody — the legend came 200 years later."
- Hook + cover idea: "A single flower bulb cost as much as a house." ↔ "ONE BULB, ONE HOUSE" / "A HOUSE FOR A FLOWER".
- Verified anchors (writer re-checks): winter 1636–37 tulip contract prices spiked in the Dutch Republic; the
  Semper Augustus was the most famous bulb (reported asking prices up to 10,000 guilders; Goldgar's receipts top out
  around 5,000 guilders, the price of a fine house in 1637); prices collapsed in February 1637 (first setback around
  3 February, Haarlem); historian Anne Goldgar (2007) found no one who went bankrupt because of the crash, and only
  37 people who paid more than 300 guilders for a bulb; the ruin story mostly comes from Charles Mackay's 1841 book.
- Paper: gurub.
- Writer's check of the facts (2026-11-23): Wikipedia "Tulip mania" (collapse in February 1637, Haarlem auction with
  no buyers; Mackay 1841; most contracts never honoured; traders met in tavern "colleges"); History.com (Goldgar found
  no one who went bankrupt; only 37 people paid more than 300 guilders for a bulb, about a skilled craftsman's yearly
  wage; top receipts 5,000 guilders, the price of a nice house in 1637); IHR Reviews in History (review of Goldgar 2007).
  Mackay's words: "Substantial merchants were reduced almost to beggary" (quoted widely). The 10,000-guilder Semper
  Augustus figure is a reported asking price, so the script says only "worth a house".
- Narration (487 chars, read aloud):
  > One tulip bulb was worth a house. In 1637, Dutch tulip prices were soaring. Bulbs still underground were sold on
  > paper, in taverns. Then, in February, the buyers vanished. We are told merchants became beggars. But that tale was
  > made famous in 1841, two centuries later. Historian Anne Goldgar searched the records. Only 37 buyers paid over a
  > year's wages for a bulb. She found not one bankruptcy. The crash was real. The ruin was a legend. Subscribe for a
  > true story like this every day.
- Cover: **ONE BULB, ONE HOUSE** ("house" amber), scene 0: the flamed tulip as tall as a canal house, both mirrored in
  the canal. Candidates tried: "Worth a house" (title touches the tulip), "This cost a house" on the still life (title
