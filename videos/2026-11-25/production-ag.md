# ag-01 — Sticky-rice mortar of the Great Wall (Shorts)

- **File:** `videos/2026-11-25/ag-01-sticky-rice-mortar.json` · `publish_at` `2026-11-25T13:00:00-05:00` · paper `yaz`
  (green summer mountains, grey brick; differs from pamuk)
- **Category:** how it was built
- **Topic:** Ming-era builders mixed cooked sticky (glutinous) rice soup into slaked-lime mortar for city walls, tombs,
  pagodas and parts of the Great Wall (VERIFY scope: say "parts of the Great Wall" / "Ming walls", not the whole wall).
  2010 study by Bingjian Zhang's team (Zhejiang University, Accounts of Chemical Research — VERIFY): amylopectin in the
  rice acts as an inhibitor that controls calcium carbonate crystal growth → compact, tough, water-resistant mortar.
  Legend/claims (VERIFY): some walls so strong that weeds can't grow / bulldozers struggled — only if sourced. The team
  then used sticky-rice mortar to restore old buildings. ~1,500-year-old technique (VERIFY: first used ~ 5th–6th c. AD?).
- **Viewer should feel:** "A kitchen ingredient is part of why those walls still stand."
- **Hook + cover:** first line ≈ "Parts of the Great Wall are glued with rice." ↔ cover "GLUED WITH / **RICE**" or
  "THE WALL'S / SECRET **RICE**" or "**RICE** HOLDS IT" (≥ 3 candidates; single subject: a big brick wall segment / one
  watchtower with a bowl of rice, or a single brick with a white mortar seam).
- **Narration draft (writer verifies and polishes to 400–500 chars, hard max 550; one flowing text):**
  Parts of the Great Wall are held together with rice. Ming builders boiled sticky rice into a thin soup and stirred it
  into lime mortar. It sounds like a kitchen trick, but it worked. In 2010, chemists in China finally found out why. A
  starch in the rice, amylopectin, slows the lime as it hardens, so its crystals grow small and packed tight. The result
  is tougher and shrugs off rain. Today restorers mix the same rice into new mortar. Subscribe for how the past was built.
- **Shot ideas:** hero: watchtower on a mountain ridge, brick wall snaking away (cover) → cauldron of rice boiling over a

  the wall, water beading off → restorer with a trowel today → title card. ≥ 3 types, ≥ 1 close-up.
