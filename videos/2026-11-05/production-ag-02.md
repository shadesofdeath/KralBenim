### Facts checked (writer)
- 36 barrels of gunpowder in the vault beneath the House of Lords; the man calling himself "John Johnson" (Guy Fawkes)
  was seized by Sir Thomas Knyvet around 11 pm–midnight on 4 November 1605 (The National Archives blog, Levinus Muncke's
  account of 7 Nov 1605). The Monteagle letter: unsigned, "written in a disguised hand without date or name", handed to
  Monteagle's footman in the street (National Archives); delivered 26 October 1605 = ten days before the 5 Nov State
  Opening (standard date). Its words "they shall receive a terrible blow this parliament" (letter text, well known).
- UK Parliament FAQ: cellar searched at midnight on 4 Nov; Act 3 James I c.1 made 5 Nov a day of thanksgiving (until
  1859); fireworks and bonfires still mark it. UK Parliament Living Heritage: the Yeomen of the Guard still ceremonially
  search the cellars before the State Opening (not narrated, budget).
- "The powder had decayed": Exploding the Legend (2005) says it was labelled "decayed" only as unfit for infantry use but
  could still explode → myth **not used**. 2005 test numbers not used (budget). No execution details.
- web fetch of en.wikipedia / www.parliament.uk direct URLs was blocked by the tool's provenance rule; the parliament
  pages were read on their webtest.parliament.uk mirror (same path) and are cited at www.parliament.uk.
