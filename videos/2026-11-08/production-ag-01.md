### Facts (verified)
- 16 July 1962: Siffre (born 1939, so 23) descended into the Scarasson abyss, an underground glacier in the Maritime Alps, about
  130 m below the surface; planned end 14 September; no clock, no daylight (IFLScience; Wikipedia; Gizmodo: "23-year-old").
- He phoned the surface team when he woke, ate and slept; they did not tell him the time (Gizmodo; Cabinet interview).
- Counting to 120 (what he felt were 120 seconds) took him five minutes (IFLScience; Gizmodo).
- When the team called on 14 September to say it was over, he believed it was 20 August (IFLScience) → 25 days "lost".
- His sleep-wake cycle stayed close to a day (~24.5 h) — body clock without the sun (IFLScience). Narration keeps this general.
- Dropped: exact exit day (he came up a few days later; sources differ 62/63 days), the 1972 Texas 48-hour cycle.
