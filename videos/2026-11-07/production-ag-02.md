### Facts (verified)
- 1834: Dr. John Cook Bennett (Willoughby medical college, Ohio) claimed tomatoes treated diarrhea, indigestion/dyspepsia, bilious attacks;
  he published tomato recipes incl. tomato ketchup (Atlas Obscura; Mental Floss; Popular Science).
- 1837: Archibald Miles (Cleveland) sold "Dr. Miles' Compound Extract of Tomato"; Guy R. Phelps sold Compound Tomato Pills; the two fought
  in the newspapers ~2 years; Phelps: Miles had "about as much claim to the title of doctor as my horse, and no more" (Atlas Obscura).
- Pills: none of the analysed pills contained tomato, they were essentially laxatives (Atlas Obscura); "many contained no actual tomato",
  some cut with laxatives (Mental Floss) → narration says "most had no tomato at all" + "Laxatives".
- Word origin (description only): Hokkien kê-tsiap, fermented fish sauce; mushroom/walnut ketchups in 1700s Britain (Smithsonian).
- Wording note: "Ketchup was once sold as medicine" follows the common framing (PopSci "Ketchup was once a diarrhea cure"); strictly, what was
  sold were tomato/"ketchup" pills and tomatoes promoted as cures, while Bennett promoted ketchup recipes.
- Sources: https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/tomato-pill · https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/620380/when-tomatoes-were-marketed-medicine ·
  https://www.popsci.com/science/ketchup-history-medicine/ · https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/ketchup-fishy-origins-180963511/
