### Narration (one piece, 418 chars after round 1)
These dads starve for almost four months. In the Antarctic winter, at minus 40 degrees, each one keeps an egg on his
feet for two months. With Mom at sea, he loses nearly half his weight. So how does he survive? Thousands pack together,
and every minute, the huddle shuffles a few centimeters. Slowly, the freezing edge keeps changing faces. Nobody gets
left out in the cold. Subscribe for a wild true story every day.

### Verified facts
- Egg on the feet under a brood pouch, 62–67 days (avg 64), down to −40 °C, males fast through it: Guinness World Records; WWF UK (65–75 days).
- Fast ~115 days (Kooyman, JEB, via Popular Science); WWF "up to four months" → "almost four months".
- Weight loss: WWF "almost half", Cool Antarctica ~40 % → "nearly half".
- Huddle: steps of 5–10 cm in travelling waves every 30–60 s at −43 °C (Australian Antarctic Division on Zitterbart et al. 2011, PLoS ONE) → "every minute … a few centimeters". Birds rotate between windward edge and middle (Cool Antarctica).
- −40 is the same in °C and °F, so "minus 40 degrees" is right for every viewer.
