# ag-01 — Pyrrhus: the king who won himself into ruin (Short)

- **File:** `ag-01-pyrrhic-victory.json` · `publish_at` `2026-11-28T13:00:00-05:00` · paper `gurub`
- **Category:** empires and battles
- **Story:** 280 BC. Tarentum calls Pyrrhus of Epirus, the best general of his age, against Rome. He lands with war
  elephants the Romans have never seen; at Heraclea the elephants break the Roman cavalry and he wins. In 279 BC at
  Asculum he wins again — but loses most of the officers and veterans he brought, men he cannot replace, while Rome
  simply raises new legions. Plutarch (Pyrrhus 21) gives his line: "If we are victorious in one more battle with the
  Romans, we shall be utterly ruined." That is where "Pyrrhic victory" comes from. Coda: beaten at Beneventum (275 BC),
  he leaves Italy and dies in a street fight in Argos (272 BC), knocked down by a roof tile thrown by an old woman
  (Plutarch 34). Writer decides whether the coda fits the budget; numbers only if sourced (troop and elephant numbers
  vary between sources: say "about twenty elephants" only if verified, otherwise no number).
- **Viewer feels at the end:** the cold irony that winning can be the thing that kills you, and the word they use
  every day now has a face.
- **Hook / cover idea:** first line "He won, and it ruined him." ↔ cover "HE WON. / HE LOST." (highlight LOST) or
  "ONE MORE / WIN", "WON TO / DEATH" — at least 3 candidates. Single subject: one war elephant against a sunset sky,
  or the king alone on a battlefield of fallen banners.
- **Ending:** punchline (e.g. the roof tile, or "Rome lost the battles. Pyrrhus lost the war.") + one short natural

# ag-02 — Sweden's February 30th, 1712 (Short)

- **File:** `ag-02-february-30-sweden.json` · `publish_at` `2026-11-28T19:00:00-05:00` · paper `keten`
- **Category:** history's strangest moments
- **Story:** Sweden decided in 1699/1700 to move from the Julian to the Gregorian calendar gradually: drop every leap
  day from 1700 to 1740 and drift over by eleven days. 1700's leap day was skipped — then the Great Northern War came
  and 1704 and 1708 kept their leap days. Sweden was now one day off from everyone: from the Julian countries and the
  Gregorian ones. In 1712 King Charles XII gave up and went back to the old calendar by adding the missing day: that
  February had a 29th and a 30th. Sweden finally switched properly in 1753 (17 February followed by 1 March). Writer
  verifies every date (Royal Museums Greenwich, Britannica, Swedish sources).
- **Viewer feels at the end:** delighted disbelief — a whole country lived a day that "doesn't exist", because a
  plan to save trouble created more of it.
- **Hook / cover idea:** first line "In 1712, Sweden had a February 30th." ↔ cover "FEBRUARY / 30" (highlight 30) or
  "A DAY THAT / NEVER WAS", "30 FEBRUARY". Single subject: one calendar page / almanac leaf with "30" on a winter
  Stockholm, or the king's seal on a decree.
- **Ending:** punchline (e.g. "It is the only February 30th that ever really happened."— only if true: the Soviet
  1930–31 calendar also "had" 30 February on paper; phrase carefully) + one short subscribe line.
