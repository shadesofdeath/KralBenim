### Facts checked (writer)
- Taurids = debris of Comet 2P/Encke; known for fireballs; "Halloween fireballs"; active roughly Sept–Dec, peaks Oct–Nov
  (NASA Science 2P/Encke; RMG; EarthSky). 2026 peaks: North Taurids ~12 Nov (EarthSky + RMG agree); South Taurids ~5 Nov
  per EarthSky but RMG says 10 Oct → narration avoids an exact South Taurid date ("every November", "this week").
- Encke orbit 3.30 years (NASA: "shortest orbital period of any known comet"; EarthSky: shortest of any major comet;
  Wikipedia notes faint main-belt comet 311P is shorter) → narration only says "every 3.3 years"; description says "major comet".
- Méchain first saw it 17 Jan 1786; Encke linked the 1786/1795/1805/1818 sightings, published 1819, predicted 1822 return
  (Wikipedia; NASA).
- Speed ~17 miles (27 km) per second vs Perseids ~37 mi/s (Space.com, RMG) → "slow". Typical meteoroids sand-grain size,
  Taurid debris larger, "pebble-sized" (Astronomy Now quoting D. Asher, Armagh). Not used: Tunguska, swarm years.
- URLs: https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/comets/2p-encke · https://earthsky.org/astronomy-essentials/taurid-meteors-all-you-need-to-know/ ·
  https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/space-astronomy/taurid-meteor-shower-when-where-see-it-uk ·

comet, named for Johann Encke, who cracked its orbit. It loops the Sun every 3.3 years, and sheds rock every lap. A
normal meteor is a grain of sand. But Taurid pieces can be pebbles, so they burn longer and brighter. See one this week?
