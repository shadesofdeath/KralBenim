## Facts (verified)
- Wikipedia (Caroline Herschel): typhus at age ten stunted her growth, "never grew more than 4 feet 3 inches"; her mother
  wanted her trained as a house servant ("household drudgery"); first comet on 1 August 1786 "while her brother was away and
  she was using his telescope"; 1787 King George III granted her an annual salary of £50; eight comets 1786–1797.
- Royal Museums Greenwich: demanding mother kept her doing chores; first comet 1786 (C/1786 P1); £50 salary from George III in
  1787 as William's assistant; eight comets, last in 1797; "first paid female astronomer".
- Max Planck Society: typhus as a child stunted her growth; mother saw her as household help; 1 Aug 1786 comet; eight comets;
  "first woman to be paid for her contribution to science" / "first female professional astronomer".
- MacTutor (St Andrews): comet C/1786 P1 on 1 August 1786; pension of 50 pounds the following year; first woman paid as a
  professional astronomer; eight comets.
- NOT used: "first woman to discover a comet" (Maria Kirch found one in 1702, credited to her husband) and the Euclid-comet
  headline details (kept out of the narration to save budget; it lives only in this brief).
