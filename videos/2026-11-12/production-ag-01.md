- **Topic:** who invented daylight saving time. George Vernon Hudson, New Zealand entomologist / post-office clerk in Wellington,
  proposed a seasonal two-hour shift in 1895 (paper to the Wellington Philosophical Society) because he wanted more daylight after
  work to collect insects. William Willett (UK builder) independently pushed it from 1907 ("The Waste of Daylight"). First nationwide
  use: Germany (and Austria-Hungary) on 30 April 1916, during WWI, to save coal. Benjamin Franklin's 1784 letter (Journal de Paris)
  was satire about waking up earlier (cannons, shutter tax) and did NOT propose changing clocks — common myth, do not repeat it as
  fact (you may say "it wasn't Franklin" if it fits the budget). VERIFY every date/number on ≥ 2 sources (Te Ara / NZ History,

A bug collector stole your hour of sleep. British Columbia just quit changing clocks, but it began in 1895. George Hudson,
a New Zealand postal clerk, wanted daylight after work to hunt insects. So he said: move the clocks two hours. Scientists
laughed. Then war came. In 1916, Germany did it to save coal, and the world copied. All because one man wanted more time
with his bugs. Subscribe for a true story like this every day.

Fact check (≥ 2 sources each): Hudson = Wellington post office clerk + insect collector, shift work gave him daylight
leisure (Te Ara, Wikipedia); paper to the Wellington Philosophical Society 16 Oct 1895, two-hour shift (Te Ara, Nautilus,
Wikipedia); ridiculed ("wholly unscientific and impracticable", Te Ara + Nautilus); Germany (+ Austria-Hungary) 30 Apr 1916,
first countrywide DST, to save coal (Western Front Association, timeanddate); others followed within weeks (timeanddate,
Nautilus: England 1916, US 1918); B.C. skipped the 1 Nov 2026 fall-back (CBC headline, Narcity). Franklin myth not used.
