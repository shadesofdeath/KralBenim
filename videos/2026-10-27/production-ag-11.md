## Facts checked

- ~2% Neanderthal DNA in people of non-African descent: Prüfer et al. 2017 (Science, Vindija genome) and Reich give about 2%;
  the 2010 draft (Green et al.) gave 1–4%. "About two percent" is safe. Africans carry much less (~0.3%, Chen et al. 2020),
  so the script says "if your roots are outside Africa".
- Extinction about 40,000 years ago: Higham et al. 2014 (Nature, 40 sites, radiocarbon).
- 2010 draft genome from bones of three Neanderthals from Vindija Cave, Croatia; Svante Pääbo, Nobel Prize 2022.
- Interbreeding about 47,000–65,000 years ago (kicker says "~50,000 years ago"); 1,500 generations is a low estimate
  (47,000 years / ~29 years per generation ≈ 1,600), so "over 1,500" holds.
- No claims about what particular genes "do" (avoids overclaiming).
- Where they met is not known exactly; the scene shows a Middle Eastern plain (the most common view) without naming a place.
