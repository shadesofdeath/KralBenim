- **Topic:** The "orphan tsunami" of January 1700: waves hit Japan's Pacific coast with no earthquake felt there; three
  centuries later Japanese records, ghost forests in Washington (tree rings ending in 1699) and Native oral histories

## Verified facts
- Japanese records: waves on the 8th–9th day of the 12th month of Genroku 12 (27–28 Jan 1700); Kuwagasaki (Miyako): 13 houses
  destroyed by the flooding and about 20 more in a fire that followed; no earthquake felt in Japan (USGS PP1707; Wikipedia summary).
- Quake: about 9 p.m. Pacific time, 26 Jan 1700 (Satake et al. 1996, Nature); magnitude ~9 (8.7–9.2; preferred 9.0, Satake, Wang & Atwater 2003).
- Ghost forests: red cedars killed when coastal land dropped into the tidal zone (e.g. Copalis River, Washington); outermost rings date to 1699.
- Oral histories: Huu-ay-aht (Pachena Bay) account of a winter-night earthquake and wave that destroyed the villages; Makah, Quileute accounts.
- Wave crossed the Pacific in roughly 10 hours (not stated in the narration).

## Narration (one continuous text, written first; rewritten after round 1: clues first, reveal last)
A tsunami hit Japan with no earthquake. It was 1700. Homes washed away, but no one felt a tremor. So where did this
orphan wave come from? The clues? Across the ocean. In Washington, cedars died as the land sank, and their last rings
stop in 1699. Native stories tell of a winter night flood. So run the wave's timing backwards, and you land on a
magnitude nine quake, about nine at night, January 26. Japan kept the receipt for a quake it never felt. Subscribe for
a true story like this every day.

(499 characters. The hour comes from working backwards from the wave's arrival times in Japan: Satake et al. 1996.)
